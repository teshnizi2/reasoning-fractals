What am I looking at?

The picture

A small recurrent model (EqR) solves Sudoku by iterating: it keeps a hidden state, the latent, and updates it for 24 loops, reading a candidate grid off the latent after every loop. Normally the state starts as random noise.

Here the Sudoku is fixed and only the initial latent changes. Two directions in the 49,664-dimensional latent space span a flat sheet; every pixel is one point on that sheet, i.e. one starting thought.

Colour is how many loops the decoded grid kept changing before it settled. Dark indigo: settled almost at once. Mint: still revising late.

Basins, and why they are fractal

All starts that end in the same answer form a basin. Where two basins meet, the model's route depends on which side of a saddle it started, so the boundary is where a tiny nudge changes the story.

Zoom in and the boundary never smooths out: new filaments keep appearing at every scale. That is a fractal basin boundary, and it means sensitivity: a one-pixel change of the first thought can add or remove many loops of thinking.
